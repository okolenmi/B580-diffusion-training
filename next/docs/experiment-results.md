## Timesteps per image (regions)
- Several timesteps per image in one step (3 regions) beat one timestep per image on composition. The `strata` layout with per-region gradient normalisation (`equal`) is the best plain route so far: fewest structural errors.
- Gradients of timestep regions are nearly orthogonal (cosine within +-0.13), so `project` and `common` (MGDA) behave like `equal`. Normalising each region's gradient to unit length is the lever; region losses differ 20-70x.
- Gradient accumulation of 4 gives 3x fewer optimizer steps and is not comparable at the same learning rate.
- `equal` costs three separate forward-backward passes per step; `mean` is one batch and one pass but does not scale the regions.

## Input perturbation
- Extra noise in the network input only, target unchanged. At the noisy end it hurts (t=950 adapter/base ratio 0.80 -> 0.95). At the clean end only it adds detail; whether it beats plain `equal` is inside the noise.

## Epsilon scaling (sampling time, no training)
- Dividing the predicted noise by 1.005-1.04 raises the finest-octave detail 3.0x -> 22.6x, but the coarse change grows too (0.092 -> 0.140). Details look out of place. Not a fix; "the model under-predicts the noise magnitude" is not the cause.

## Self-perceptual loss (Lin and Yang, arXiv 2401.00110)
- Optional extra term, the Self-perceptual loss addon of the Trainer (`weight`): frozen base network (adapter off), mid-block features of the re-noised image estimate against the true image.
- Weight 0.3 destroyed the result (adapter/base loss 2-14 at every t, finest octave 31x, coarse change 0.41). Its gradient was about 15x the plain loss's at step 1 and, after per-region normalisation, decided the direction; gradient norms then diverged (1e5 to 1e10). Weights 1 and 3 were not run.
- Likely causes: weight far too large for a LoRA; the estimate `x - sigma*eps_hat` is far outside what the network sees at high noise. The paper trains full weights, v prediction, batch 896, after MSE pretraining. Untried: weight 0.003-0.03, a bounded estimate, gradient-norm-matched weighting.

## Learning rate (resolution 768, rendered at 1024)
- Mean loss over the last 100 steps: 0.1028 (1e-4), 0.1028 (1e-4 again), 0.1036 (3e-5), 0.1026 (3e-4). Adapter/base loss ratio per t differs by at most 0.01 between 1e-4 and 3e-4; all arms improve the plain loss by about 5% (23% at t=950).
- The two 1e-4 controls match to four digits in loss but differ in image metrics: finest octave 1.77 vs 1.60, spectral slope -2.56 vs -2.60, fine-power share 0.115 vs 0.103. So image-metric differences under about 10-15% are not results, even when the training loss is identical (a sampler amplifies tiny weight differences).
- 3e-5: less coarse change (0.066 vs 0.086), more fine detail (3.26), judged worse by eye: undertrained. 1e-4 and 3e-4: each better on different images, within the noise. The learning rate is not the cause of the painted look in this range.
- Speed: 768 px gave about 3.2 s per step against about 3.5 s at 1024 (0.6x the pixels): batch-1 passes are probably launch-bound, not compute-bound (not measured).

## One pass instead of three (resolution 768, rendered at 1024)
- Minutes for 300 steps: equal 18.4, mean 11.4, calibrated-10 13.1, calibrated-50 12.1. One batch of three timesteps costs about 62% of three separate passes: the saving is a third at most, not two thirds.
- Coarse change (the least noisy image metric; the two identical 1e-4 controls differed by 1.5%): equal 0.0853, calibrated-10 0.0801, calibrated-50 0.0792, mean 0.0652 (about what the undertrained 3e-5 run gave, 0.0662). It follows the order the owner judged by eye: `equal` learns the most structure, `mean` the least. Fine/coarse is the reverse: equal 0.24, calibrated 0.27, mean 0.34, since in `mean` the clean-end region, with a loss 20x larger, dominates the gradient.
- Calibrated weights are fixed ratios measured every 10 or 50 steps; `equal` normalises each step's own gradient, and the norms vary several-fold from step to step, so a fixed weight cannot follow them.
- Conclusion: `equal` stays the base. The calibrated option stays in the code (`--policy-calibrate-every`) but costs quality for a third less time. The logged loss of the calibrated arms (0.0497, 0.0424) was the balance-weighted loss, which the code no longer reports.

## First steps (resolution 768, rendered at 1024)
- `equal` 16.7 min, `warmup-50` 17.7, `ladder-6-warmup` 19.6 (the ladder's early steps run up to 6 passes). The logged losses and per-t tables are the same to the digit in all three.
- Metrics: coarse change 0.0839 / 0.0797 / 0.0801, finest octave 1.80 / 1.78 / 1.57. The warmup arms change slightly less at the coarse level and are less detailed at the finest; the owner prefers `ladder-6-warmup` by eye for the small details of the composition.
- `ladder-9` ran out of memory at step 1: the gradient of every region (162 MB each) was kept on the card, nine of them plus the stacked matrix. Fixed by keeping them in host memory.
- Owner's idea for later: 2 and 4 regions. His merge experience: with 2 examples progress is faster than with 3-4, since more examples show more errors to reject.
- Owner's reading: for `ladder-6-warmup` the improvement is in composition, almost with no trade-off; details can be fixed later, fixing them at the start of training is useless.

## Region count (resolution 768; run before the streaming change, so the minutes include the copy and pause costs)
- Minutes for 300 steps: regions-2 11.7, ladder-6 21.3, regions-4 21.6, ladder-9 22.3. Time follows the number of passes: about 5.5 min per region per 300 steps.
- Coarse change: regions-2 0.0738, ladder-9 0.0776, ladder-6 0.0800, regions-4 0.0815 (noise about 2%): more regions change the structure more, saturating at 3-4; 2 changes it about 8% less. Regions-2 also keeps the spectrum nearest the base model (slope -2.33, fine-power share 0.289 against 0.287 for the base).
- Logged losses are the same to the digit across arms with the same region count because the noise and timestep stream is seeded the same way; they differ between region counts because the stream differs, so loss columns compare only arms with equal region counts.
- The owner could not name a clear winner by eye; he is running 600 steps and a heavier ladder (warmup 100, ladder 9, 4 regions).

## Region count at 600 steps (after the streaming change)
- Minutes for 600 steps: regions-2 22.9, ladder-6 33.7, ladder-9 35.9, regions-4 40.4, ladder-9-r4 49.0. A step of 3 regions takes about 2.8 s at the end (3.3 s before the streaming change), 4 regions 3.8 s (4.3 s), 2 regions 2.1 s: about 0.93 s per pass.
- Coarse change: regions-2 0.0856, ladder-9 0.0940, ladder-6 0.0967, regions-4 0.0981, ladder-9-r4 0.0994. Finest octave: ladder-6 3.02, ladder-9 3.03, ladder-9-r4 2.40, regions-4 2.02, regions-2 1.96; sharpness against the base 4.24 / 3.97 / 3.02 / 2.70 / 3.20. The 3-region ladders keep the most fine detail; 4 regions change the structure most and lose detail.
- Mean pairwise cosine of the regions' gradients (`cos_mean`): at the start 0.13 for ladder 6 and 0.22 for ladder 9, falling to about 0.04 after 200 steps; with a final count of 4 it stays higher (0.05-0.07); with 2 it is 0.03-0.04. Adjacent timesteps pull the adapter in more similar directions, so a 4th region adds less independent signal and fits the one image harder.
- Owner's reading by eye: 3 regions is the best count, ladder 9 better than ladder 6 on the long run (a good initial directive helps), 4 regions gives more issues, ladder-9-r4 (span 200, final 4) was a failure. That arm differs from ladder 9 in two ways (final count 4, span 200), and 4 regions alone also did worse, so the final count is the likelier cause. The metrics do not separate ladder 6 from ladder 9 (within the noise).
- Owner's main finding: with 3 regions (and the ladder) more steps now improve the pictures (300 -> 600: chaos turns into real forms, e.g. a crowd in one room in sheet pd41d8c_s3), where plain training gave no progress and only more destruction beyond a few hundred steps.

## Ladder span (600 steps, 3 final regions, warmup 50)
- Span 50, 100 (control, the region-count run) and 200: coarse change 0.0940 / 0.0940 / 0.0997, finest octave 2.93 / 3.03 / 3.43, sharpness against the base 4.07 / 3.97 / 4.97. A longer ladder changes more at every level.
- Owner by eye: 50 worse than 100; 200 has some structure issues but sometimes better results; would move towards 120. Ladder 9 on another data order (`batcher.seed=7`) did a good enough job, better in some respects and worse in others than the first order, so the ladder's gain does not depend on one order. Different data order gives different logged losses (0.1003 against 0.1028) and a different probe sample, so those are not comparable across orders.
- Recommended setting so far: `--regions 3 --regions-from 9 --regions-span 120 --warmup 50`.

## Several images per pass (600 steps, 3 final regions, ladder 9 over 100 batches, warmup 50)
- Minutes: batch-2 36.3, batch-4 62.6, batch-4 with lr 2e-4 61.4; the control (batch 1, region-count test) 35.9. Two images per pass are free, four cost 1.7 times: the gain from the second image is hidden launch time, not compute.
- Logged loss over the last 100 steps 0.1014 / 0.1025 / 0.1021 (same noise stream, so comparable only roughly across batch sizes).
- Coarse change: 0.0901 / 0.0997 / 0.1052 (control 0.0940); finest octave against the base 1.38 / 2.30 / 2.44 (control 3.03); sharpness against the base 1.91 / 2.86 / 2.68 (control 3.97). The finest octave has about 10% noise, so batch-2's low value alone is not a finding.
- Probe at the end, adapter/base loss by third on the training images: batch-2 0.956 / 0.958 / 0.889, batch-4 0.945 / 0.943 / 0.875, batch-4 lr 2e-4 0.942 / 0.941 / 0.873: a bigger batch fits the training images better; the higher rate a little more. Held-out loss by timestep is the same in all three (the table in the report).
- Owner by eye: lr 2e-4 is good. A bigger batch gives less creativity but, when details are the goal, is clearly worth its cost; the two can be combined, batch 1 at the start and a bigger batch near the end (TESTING 57).

## Long run: 1200 steps, constant against cosine (resolution 768, rendered at 1024, 204 images, about 6 epochs)
- Mean loss per 150 steps is 0.0999, 0.1029, 0.0984, 0.1002, 0.1006, 0.0998, 0.1028, 0.1008 in the constant arm, the same to three digits in the cosine arm: the swings follow the data order, there is no trend after step 300. The probe ratio on training images sits at 0.950/0.937/0.880 from about step 500.
- The agreement between the regions' gradients (mean cosine) falls from 0.09 (first 150 steps) to 0.03 and 0.02 (last 300 steps): after the shared direction is learned, what is left of a step's gradient is specific to its sample and noise. The gradient norm stays flat, and Adam moves the weights by about the learning rate per step whatever the gradient size, so the later steps move the weights without lowering the loss.
- The adapter's gain sits at high t (adapter/base 0.77 at t=950, 0.95 at t<=650) and is the same on holdout images: at high noise the best prediction is the dataset's mean image for the prompt, so what is learned first and most is the dataset's shared palette, softness and layout, for every prompt.
- Cosine decay did not change that look (both arms: soft, muted, same composition); its metrics sit 15% nearer the reference, which is noise-level.
- Open: save at 300, 600, 900 and 1200 in one run to see where the look sets in; strength 0.5-0.7 on the final file to see whether it is magnitude only.

## LoRA+, gradient-aligned start, batch ramp (600 steps, resolution 768, rendered at 1024)
- All arms share the same loss to three digits and the same per-t tables (0.95 at low t, 0.77 at t=950, holdout equal to train); only the image metrics and the pictures separate them, as in the earlier rounds.
- LoRA+ 4 and 16: coarse change 0.0926 and 0.0977 against 0.0874, finest octave 3.13 and 3.08 against 2.87, fine/coarse 0.28 and 0.25 against 0.27. No cost in time. By eye both are good; the two are not told apart.
- Aligned start: 4 batches give a finest octave of 3.07 and sharpness 3.90 (baseline 2.92 and 3.74) and are very good in textures; 16 batches give 1.99 and 2.55 and are a fail by eye although the probe at step 600 is the lowest of the three. The first N batches are consumed from the data, so the ladder and the data order shift with N.
- Batch ramp: 52.9, 50.7 and 45.7 minutes for the three ramps against 36.6; finest octave 1.53, 1.87 and 2.06 against 3.00 for batch 1 at lr 2e-4. Batch 1 at lr 2e-4 is the best of the similar runs by eye.
- Warning seen in the ramp run: the oneDNN primitive cache evicts shapes already built (revisits 1.53x slower than repeats, 4.66 s against 3.05 s).

## Was the baseline's result the restart? (anchor run, item 63)
The baseline arm was cut twice and continued from the step-400 state both times (the final adapter is steps 1-400 of the first pass plus 401-600 of the last), and its pictures were the best the owner had seen at default settings. Question: does a restart change the result?
- Log evidence, weak: steps 401-463 ran three times (once uninterrupted, twice resumed) on the same batches. Losses agree to three digits and differ in the fourth (step 523: 0.2522 against 0.2523 in the low third), gradient norms agree on average (log ratio of per-region norms, uninterrupted against resumed: mean -0.010, spread 0.197; two resumed passes against each other: +0.009, 0.196) but single steps differ up to 3x (region 0 at step 407: 0.081 against 0.024 and 0.020). The kernels are not deterministic. A loss that agrees says little about the pictures, so this is not proof.
- Proof on the CPU (`check_resume`): the baseline recipe (three regions, ladder, warmup, LoRA+), the aligned start, the anchor and the batch ramp each trained straight through, and as a process stopped at step 4 followed by a new process continuing to step 8. The adapters are identical to the last bit and so are every step's losses; with the saved state withheld the test fails. So what a resume saves (adapter, optimizer moments and counter, data position, ladder, ramp, anchor) is the whole training state, in the code.
- What the CPU cannot show: a difference in the XPU's kernels or allocator after a restart. Steps after the restart were slower (2.9-3.1 s against 2.64 s) with lower card use (7.6-7.8 GB against 8.3), so the restarted process does behave differently on the card.
- First card try (200 steps, cut at 100, the lab restarted the process itself): the deterministic arms agreed with each other and with the cut one; the ordinary arm differed. By the owner's eye the deterministic pictures looked better than the ordinary one on most images. Not conclusive: 200 steps cannot be compared with the 600-step baseline, the process restarted under a lab that stayed alive (caches of the lab and the driver were warm), and one ordinary run cannot tell a restart from the spread between two ordinary runs.
- The test was redone as `resume-proof` (TESTING 75): the baseline's recipe and 600 steps, a cut at 400 that stops the whole lab (the continuation is a new lab and a new process, after a reboot if wanted), two uninterrupted ordinary runs for the spread, and the same three arms with deterministic kernels, so the pictures of ordinary and deterministic runs can be compared at the baseline's length.
- The metrics sit inside the known spread of earlier baselines with this recipe: finest octave 3.55 (earlier 2.87-3.43), sharpness against the reference 4.17 (3.4-4.24), coarse change 0.104 (0.087-0.102). Whether the pictures were luck is for the repeat to say.
- Found on the way: the toy text encoder of `check_engine` seeded its noise with Python's `hash()`, which differs per process; it made two uninterrupted runs in separate processes differ. Production code has no such use.

- Training alone plateaus (loss flat from about step 300, pictures keep improving slowly); the lever found so far is *fewer, cleaner weights*: the saved best adapter loses nothing in loss when attn2, the middle block or the input blocks are removed, and looks cleaner by eye.
- Cleaning by weight magnitude to a small share (8-15%) beat the learned gates and the component/singular variants by eye; scaling the result back up is not wanted for training. The plan is staged training in one run: train, clean to about 8% and freeze, train the rest at a lower rate, clean to a larger share, and so on (`--stage`, item 71), possibly with a different rate per part of the network (`--lr-region`, item 72) and without attn2 / the middle block (`targets`, item 70).
- Averaged weights (EMA) are dropped; the anchor loss and learned gates removed almost the whole dataset effect together with the harmful part (the weights carrying both overlap).
